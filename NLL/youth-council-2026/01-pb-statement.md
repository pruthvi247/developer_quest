Phase 1: Case Study  Challenge Scenario Horizon RetailCo is a leading consumer retail company with over 2,000 stores across metropolitan, urban, and emerging markets.Three years ago, the company launched an initiative to encourage innovation from across the organization. Employees at all levels were invited to submit ideas to improve customer experience, operational efficiency, sustainability, and business growth.The initiative generated strong participation. More than 12,000 ideas were submitted during this period. However, a recent review by the Executive Committee revealed three trends: Nearly 70% of funded ideas originated from employees based at headquarters and large regional offices, despite these locations representing less than 35% of the workforce. Less than 15% of funded ideas came from store employees, even though they interact with customers daily and represent the majority of the workforce. Several funded ideas received strong support during the evaluation stage but faced implementation challenges due to operational realities, local customer needs, or stakeholder resistance. The Executive Committee is divided on how to respond. One group believes the current process is working as intended and that the strongest ideas naturally rise to the top. Another group believes the company may be missing valuable perspectives from certain employee groups and locations. A third group believes the issue is not idea quality, but the organization's ability to evaluate, prioritize, and implement ideas effectively. The CEO has asked for recommendations. Your Task : As an advisor to the CEO, prepare a recommendation addressing the following: What do you believe is the underlying challenge facing RetailCo? What factors may be contributing to the trends observed in the case? Which stakeholder perspectives should leadership consider before making changes? What recommendations would you make to improve the effectiveness of the innovation process? Evaluation Criteria Submissions will be assessed on: Problem Solving & Root Cause Analysis Future & Business Orientation Inclusivity of Perspectives Practicality and Feasibility of Recommendations Clarity and Structure of Communication

### Core Diagnosis
What May Be Driving the Current
Trends
`Key factors:`
• Unequal access to submission tools and
dedicated time.
• Confidence gap: store staff may not see
themselves as “innovators.”
• Lack of frontline representation in
evaluation.
• Weak feedback loops discourage repeat
participation.Store employees rarely see
outcomes of submitted ideas, which
further reduces participation over time.
• Instead of storing only ideas, maintain a
centralised repository of recurring
business problems.
• Implementation ownership may be
unclear after funding.
• When success is measured by volume
and approval, the program rewards
activity. When measured by adoption
and impact, it rewards results.
• The strongest ideas may not be rising to
the top.


**Why Store Ideas Matter**

Many employees have valuable ideas but may
not even recognise them as ideas that can be
implemented across stores — what they
experience as a practical workaround ("jugaad")
is often a scalable operational improvement. The
process never invites them to see it differently.

**Evaluation Bias**

Central panels evaluation: An idea that works in
a metropolitan flagship store may fail
completely in an emerging market. A central
evaluator cannot easily distinguish this.

**Stakeholders collaboration**

Promising ideas receive funding without early
involvement of store managers, supply chain,
operations, or regional leadership.
Implementation resistance is discovered late —
after budget is committed.


**MEASURE FULL FUNNEL**
• Submission rate by role, location, region, and store type.
• Participation repeat rate. Percentage of ideas receiving feedback.
• Conversion rate from submission to shortlist to funding. Pilot success
rate.
• Detect trends by location, department, or customer segment.
• Grouping multiple ideas addressing the same underlying problem.
• Business impact from implemented ideas.


## **Recommendations**

Redesign the innovation process across 4 levers

### Access
Make it easy for every employee to contribute
#### Physical Barriers (Friction Reduction)
• Mobile-first submission platform with voice input — submit in under 60 seconds on the shop
floor
• Simple structured prompt instead of an essay: What did you notice? What would you change?
Who would it help?
• Support for local languages and shop-floor friendly format
• Innovation Ambassadors in every region to encourage participation and coach employees
#### Mental Barriers (Confidence & Direction)
• Management publishes focused innovation briefs each quarter — specific areas where ideas
are actively needed (e.g., "How can we reduce checkout wait times?", "Ideas to improve
sustainability in packaging", "Store inventory management improvements"). This tells
employees: your idea is already being looked for.
• Provide idea starter templates and a library of anonymised past submissions — including ideas
that were refined and funded — so employees can see what a good idea looks like without
needing business-case writing skills
• Show examples of ideas that started small and rough but were developed into funded
initiatives, normalising imperfect first submissions

### **Evaluation**

Judge ideas on merit, customer value and feasibility

• Two-stage review process: Frontline validation → Strategic assessment

• Diverse evaluation panels including store and regional representatives

• Idea Mentoring to refine raw insights before formal evaluation

• Blind first-pass screening where possible format

• Provide feedback and learning summaries to all idea submitters

### Experimentation
Pilot before large-scale funding
• Adopt a “pilot-first” mindset – test promising ideas in 5–10 stores
• Gather real operational feedback before full rollout
• Focus on quick validation of customer value and feasibility

### **Execution**

Assign ownership and measure outcomes

• Clear ownership assigned for every funded idea
• Track progress at 30, 60, and 90 days with defined success metrics
• Establish Innovation Champions across stores
• Celebrate and publicly share store-originated success stories
