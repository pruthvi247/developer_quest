# What I Would Tell The CEO

>**The debate among executives is focused on the wrong question.**
It is not:
- Inclusion versus merit.
- Headquarters versus stores.
- Idea quality versus execution.

The real issue is that RetailCo has built a system that is optimized for collecting ideas, not for discovering customer truths and scaling successful innovations.

My recommendation would be:

1. Put customer proximity at the center of innovation.
2. Make idea evaluation blind and data-driven.
3. Fund many small experiments instead of a few large projects.
4. Build continuous innovation mechanisms into store operations.
5. Measure implemented business impact, not idea volume.

# Steve jobs

==**“What should innovation feel like if RetailCo were designing it today from scratch?”**==

==RetailCo has built an innovation process that appears to reward **proposal production**, while the business actually needs a system that produces **exceptional customer experiences**.==

==idea which works should them be made as a system that works or reused==

The present process may be asking:

- Who submitted the strongest business case?
- Which presentation received the highest score?
- Which idea has the most executive support?
- Which proposal appears most complete?

A Jobs-inspired process would ask:

- What customer experience are we trying to create?
- What is unnecessarily complicated today?
- Which idea would make a customer say, “Of course, why wasn’t it always this way?”
- Can employees explain the idea simply?
- Can RetailCo deliver the complete experience exceptionally well?


## Jobs-inspired question

Do not ask:

> “How can we improve checkout efficiency?”

Ask:

> “If we could completely reinvent the final five minutes of a customer’s visit, what should those five minutes feel like?”

That question encourages employees to imagine the outcome before defending an existing process.


## Redesign the submission experience

Store employees should be able to record an idea in under five minutes using:

- A voice note
- A short video
- Three structured questions
- A photograph of the problem
- A customer quote with personal details removed
- A store manager-assisted submission

The three initial questions could be:

1. **What did you observe?**
2. **Why does it matter to the customer?**
3. **What would a better experience look like?**

The employee should not initially be required to calculate ROI, create a presentation, or design an implementation roadmap. Specialists can help develop those elements later.

The core design principle is:

> **Do not confuse an employee’s ability to prepare a business case with the value of the customer insight.**


## Lane 1: Fix It Now

For small, reversible store improvements:

- Signage
- Product placement
- Queue management
- Basic customer communication
- Simple workflow changes

These should be handled locally with limited bureaucracy.

## Lane 2: Test and Learn

For ideas requiring evidence:

- New service formats
- Local assortment changes
- Digital assistance
- New staffing patterns
- Modified returns or checkout processes

These receive small pilot budgets and clear success measures.

## Lane 3: Reinvent the Experience

For a small number of strategic opportunities:

- A fundamentally new store journey
- A distinctive omnichannel service
- A major loyalty experience
- A new retail business model
- A differentiated local-market proposition


Find the "insanely great" customer problem
The five-question test
1. Is this real human problem
2. is the problem important
3. is the proposed experience dramatically better
4. Is it simple for the customer

Every significant evaluation team should include:

- One or more store employees
- A store manager
- A customer experience or design specialist
- An operations representative
- Relevant technology or supply-chain specialists
- A commercial or finance partner
- A single accountable owner

## Give frontline employees decision rights

Do not invite store employees only to provide feedback after the concept has been designed.

They should have the authority to say:

- “Customers will not understand this.”
- “This creates extra work during peak hours.”
- “The process assumes staffing that stores do not have.”
- “This works in metropolitan stores but not in emerging markets.”
- “Customers here behave differently.”
- “The training requirement has been underestimated.”

A store representative should be able to pause progression to the next stage if the pilot has not demonstrated operational feasibility.

Instead of slides , make it experience/story telling
